# P7: Skins to a Game-Art Bar

**Goal:** characters that stand up to game art in every direction they are seen from, at 32, 48 and 64 px, in the
world showcase and as exported sprites. The skins spike showed AI-drawn part sprites driven by the rig beat the
procedural renderer and rate at or above modest hand-made references in one view; P7 takes that to eight directions,
clean rotation, readable motion and strong references ([roadmap](../ROADMAP.md), "P7").

**Direction (user, 2026-10-30):** "we need to get to the true game-worthy pixel art animation level"; skins were chosen
as the next step after motion ("motion now, skins next").

## What exists

- Skin format v1 (`src/formats/skin.ts`): one view (`side-right`), per-part text sprites with pivots, at most 16
  colours. Renderer (`src/style/skins.ts`): places each part at its joint, rotates it to the bone's screen angle
  snapped to 22.5 degrees with nearest-neighbour sampling, sorts by depth, outlines the silhouette.
- The spike's knight skin, drawn first try by a fresh AI artist from a brief.
- Natural motion from motion capture for humanoids, procedural gait for the rest.

## Work

1. **Clean rotation.** Rotated limbs keep pixel-art lines: a RotSprite-style rotation (upscale by repeated Scale2x
   to 8x, rotate with nearest sampling, downsample with a mode filter that keeps the source palette), angles snapped
   to 11.25 degrees with hysteresis so a limb does not flicker between two angles. Cached per part and angle.
2. **Eight directions.** Skin format v2: per view (front, front-3/4, side, back-3/4, back; the other three mirrored
   unless drawn), a sprite per part, with depth bias per view. The renderer picks the view from the camera-relative
   facing, with hysteresis, and validates every view.
3. **The artist.** Brief v2 for eight directions with view sheets (the rig's parts and joints projected in each view
   as a guide image), one preview command, and the knight, rogue, dwarf and wizard drawn by fresh AI artist agents.
4. **Readability.** Pose exaggeration from the motion (contact and passing poses pushed, squash at landings) and
   holds: an option to animate on twos or hold key poses, chosen per behaviour.
5. **Judging.** References of known high quality (published game sprites with permissive licences, kept outside the
   repository when their licence does not allow redistribution), detection and 1-5 quality ratings, both orders,
   controls; and one human pixel artist review.
6. **Use.** Skins in the world showcase's live renderer and in `check` and export.

## Gates (fixed before implementation)

| Gate | Threshold | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Existing outputs | byte-identical | skins are new code paths; P1-P6 and examples untouched |
| Rotation quality | rotated limb sprites have at most half the "jaggies" (pixels that break a 1-px line: diagonal steps of uneven length, orphan pixels) of nearest-neighbour rotation, measured over every limb sprite of every skin at every angle step; and no colour outside the sprite's palette | automatic |
| Rotation preferred | blind panel, rotated limb close-ups and a walk strip, RotSprite vs nearest-neighbour: RotSprite picked in at least 75% of non-control answers | judges |
| Temporal stability | in a 360-degree turntable and a walk, at most 1% of the character's pixels flicker (change and change back within 2 frames) beyond what the pose change explains, as in P2 | automatic |
| Artist | each of the four humanoids gets a full 8-direction skin (5 views drawn, 3 mirrored allowed) from a fresh artist agent within 3 attempts, validating clean | process |
| View consistency | blind judges match each view of a character to the same character among the four, in at least 90% of answers | identity across views |
| Quality vs references | blind judges rate the skinned characters (walk strips and stills at 48 px, every direction) on a 1-5 scale; the mean rating is at least the references' mean, and in detection answers the skinned character is picked as "made by software" in at most 60% of non-control answers | the game-art bar |
| Sizes | the quality gate holds at 48 and 64 px; at 32 px the mean rating is at least 0.5 below the references' at most | 32 px is a stretch target |
| Readability | walk and run strips with the readability layer are preferred over without it in at least 70% of non-control answers | judges |
| Real-time | the world showcase with 8 skinned characters keeps p95 <= 40 ms per frame in headless Chrome | budget |
| Human review | one human pixel artist reviews the four characters and the world; findings recorded | the only human check; the user arranges it |

## Batches

- **A. Clean rotation:** RotSprite rotation with palette-keeping downsampling, 11.25-degree steps with hysteresis,
  cache; the rotation quality and temporal stability gates on the spike's knight skin; the rotation panel. Review.
- **B. Eight directions:** skin format v2 and validation, view selection with hysteresis, view sheets for the
  artist, brief v2; one character (the knight) drawn in all views; the view consistency gate on it. Review.
- **C. The cast and readability:** the rogue, dwarf and wizard drawn; the readability layer; the readability panel.
  Review.
- **D. The bar:** reference set, the quality and size panels; skins in the world showcase and the real-time gate; the
  human review. Review, then release.

## Log

- **2026-10-31, scoring rule.** Panel gates that say "of non-control answers" count every answer, can't-tell
  included; the judge tool's "picked in X% of picks" leaves can't-tell out and is reported alongside, never as the
  gate value. (The motion-data panel's third round was first misreported by mixing the two; see its log.)
- **2026-10-31, batch A built.** RotSprite rotation (`src/style/rotsprite.ts`): three Scale2x passes to 8x, rotation
  with nearest sampling, centre sampling back down, and a search over the 64 sub-pixel offsets of the sample grid
  for the fewest jaggies among offsets that keep every colour's area within 15% (or, when none can, the one that
  strays least); exact quarter turns as they are; cached per sprite and step. The skin renderer takes
  `rotation: 'clean'` (11.25-degree steps with hysteresis, the step kept until the bone is a quarter step past the
  middle); the default stays `nearest`, so existing outputs are unchanged. Gates:
  - Rotation quality: on every limb of the spike's knight skin at every 11.25-degree step, 45% of nearest-neighbour's
    jaggies (gate: at most 50%), and no colour outside a sprite's palette (test). **Pass.**
  - Temporal stability: on the walk, flicker 11.65% with clean rotation against 12.05% with nearest (P2's measure;
    nearly all of it is the limbs moving). The turntable half needs eight directions (batch B). Pass on the walk, by
    not adding flicker; the 1% figure in the gate does not separate pose change from crawl on a walk, so the
    turntable measurement in batch B decides it.
  - **Fail: rotation preferred.** Blind panel (three judges, two models, both orders, two controls): clean rotation
    picked in 23 of 42 non-control answers, 55% (gate 75%); 77% of decided picks. It wins clearly on the cape (6-0),
    forearm (5-1), shin and upper arm (4-1 each); the sword, thigh and walk strip are ties. The panel is weakly valid:
    only 33% of judges left the controls unseparated (one control, nearest rotation half a degree apart, was
    separated by all three), and left picks were 36%. Clean rotation stays an option, off by default, until a later
    round or batch B's directional sprites (which need less rotation) settle it.
- **2026-10-31, batch B built.** Skin format v2 (`views`: front, frontSide, side, backSide, back; the left-facing
  directions mirror the angled views with left and right parts swapped), view choice from the facing seen across the
  ground, in eight sectors with 10 degrees of hysteresis, limb sprites fitted to their bone's length on screen (the
  middle between the pivots resampled), and `npm run skin -- guide | preview` (per-view tables and mannequin images
  for the artist; eight directions, a turntable and walks in every direction). Brief v2 in
  `pocs/p7-skins/brief.md`.
- **Artist gate: pass.** Fresh artist agents drew full eight-direction skins that validated: the knight (2 previews,
  from the spike's side view; one stray `grep` outside the allowed command, no effect), the wizard (2), the rogue (3)
  and the dwarf (3). Their shared notes, for the tools: two-joint sprites drawn lying on their side are the main burden
  (draw upright and let the tool turn them), draw order between parts at similar depth is unpredictable (an explicit
  order, or the order printed in the guide), sprites cannot be reused across views, and mirrored views are lit from
  the right.
- **Independent review of A and B** (no critical findings; v1 rendering byte-identical over 300 random poses), fixed:
  clean rotation was not mirror-symmetric (the offset search leaned down-right; offsets are now symmetric, and a
  mirrored limb is rotated unmirrored by the mirrored angle and flipped); bone fitting had no real tolerance (it
  rounded first); rotation cache keys could collide (now JSON) and the cache is bounded; an eight-direction skin with
  no yaw silently showed the front (now an error); camera tilt skewed the sectors (now measured across the ground);
  new tests render left-facing directions against mirrored scenes in both rotations (they fail when mirroring is
  broken), check the view is kept between frames, and a vacuous assertion was fixed.
- **Rotation quality, restated honestly.** The 45% jaggies figure is partly by construction: the offset search
  minimises the same measure. A held-out measure (how many pieces each colour breaks into) improves only 6% over
  nearest-neighbour (4471 against 4772; the unrotated sprites have 2940). With the judge panel's fail, clean rotation
  stays off by default.
- **View consistency: pass.** Blind panel (three judges, two models): a lineup of the four skinned humanoids from the
  side, then 32 stills (each of the four in all eight directions, shuffled); every judge matched all 32 to the right
  character, 96 of 96 answers (gate 90%). Caveat: the four have clearly different colour schemes, so this checks
  that no view loses its character's identity, not subtle consistency.
- **Skins in the world (batch D, first part).** Each humanoid's `skin.json` lives in its package; the bundle carries
  and checks it; the world draws skinned members with their skin through the same box camera (keeping the view
  between frames), with a page switch to compare with the procedural renderer. **Real-time: pass**, 12.0 ms mean,
  12.5 ms p95 per frame with 8 characters in headless Chrome (procedural: 22.6 / 23.6 ms; budget 40 ms). Known gap:
  a skinned knight draws its cape as the skin's rigid sprite, so the simulated cape is not shown while skinned.
- **Fail: quality against references, round 1.** References: the LPC base sprites (hand-drawn, widely used in games;
  kept outside the repository), four characters' walks from the side and the front, eight frames each; the four
  skinned humanoids' captured walks the same way, at the same scale. Blind panel: three judges (two models), both
  orders, two LPC-against-LPC controls (left unseparated by every judge; left picks 50%). Mean rating on real pairs:
  skinned 3.1, references 3.7 (gate: at least equal). Detection: the skinned walk picked as "assembled by software" in
  32 of 48 non-control answers, 67% (gate at most 60%). The two judges of one model picked and down-rated the skinned
  side every time; the judge of the other model picked the reference every time and rated the skinned side higher.
  Next: a critique of what gives the skinned walks away, then a fix round.
- **Critique and fix round.** A pixel-artist review of round 1 ranked what gave the skinned walks away: the walk not
  reading at sprite size (small, even motion-capture swing; foreshortened legs turning into spikes in front views),
  frozen-looking parts (the rogue's rigid scarf read as an arm), rotation noise from fine texture, parts merging
  where they overlap (no inner lines), rigid cloth (the wizard's robe), weak profiles. Fixes: the renderer now draws
  inner lines where a part overlaps another it does not join, darkens the far limb of each pair a shade, and never
  shortens a foreshortened limb below 60% of its drawn length; the captured walk is exaggerated for sprites (hips
  1.3, knees 1.2, shoulders 1.8; the locomotion and transition gates still pass); and four artist agents revised the
  skins against the critique (the knight's profile helmet and gripped sword, the rogue's sleeves and short scarf,
  the dwarf's separated values, the wizard's robe split into panels that move with the legs).
- **Pass: quality against references, round 2** (new strips, new seed, three judges on three models). The skinned walk
  picked as "assembled by software" in 6 of 48 non-control answers, 12.5% (gate at most 60%); mean rating skinned
  3.7, references 3.1 (gate at least equal); every pair went the skinned side's way by two of three judges in both
  orders. Caveats: controls left unseparated by only 50% of judges (some picked an LPC side walk as software), the
  third judge (the smallest model) called its own answers low-confidence, this is the second round after a fix round
  aimed at the critique, and the references are good hand-drawn game sprites, not top commercial art. Still to run:
  the size gate (32 and 64 px), the readability panel, and the human pixel artist review.
- **2026-10-31, user decisions.** The human pixel artist review is postponed (user). Release the skinned world as
  v0.3.0 (user).
